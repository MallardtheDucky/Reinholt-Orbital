From a repair counter to a constellation

Magnus Reinholt grew up above the family's electronics shop in Denver, soldering radios beside his father and listening to his grandfather Anton, who had arrived from Germany with a toolbox and a talent for fixing things other people had given up on. After studying electrical engineering, he ran the family business for several years before concluding that the next great communications network would be built in orbit.

In 2003 he founded Reinholt Orbital with a dozen engineers and a rented warehouse. His rule was simple: build everything in-house and sell it for less. That approach produced the satellites, rockets and terminals that now connect millions of homes, thousands of aircraft and tens of thousands of ships.

Reinholt rarely gives interviews and avoids conferences, preferring to spend time at the Denver factory floor and launch control. Colleagues describe him as exacting, loyal to long-serving staff and unusually fond of contracts that are clear and fair. He is known for his belief that reliability is the only brand that matters.

"A signal should arrive the same way whether you are in Manhattan or in a village with no road. That is the whole business."

Magnus Reinholt, Founder's Letter
